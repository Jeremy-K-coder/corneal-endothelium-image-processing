# Computational characterization of in vivo calcium-signal dynamics in the mouse corneal endothelium across age
Corneal endothelial loss and dysfunction remains difficult to detect before irreversible structural damage occurs because current clinical assessment relies predominantly on structural indicators such as cell density, morphology, and corneal edema, which may not detect early functional impairment at a potentially reversible stage. Calcium-signal dynamics may provide a complementary functional measure of corneal endothelial health. For example, a multicenter study in Germany, in 2022 used single-cell fluorescence calcium imaging to examine Transient Receptor Potential Vanilloid 4 (TRPV4)-mediated calcium responses in cultured human corneal endothelial cells; the study found that pharmacological stimulation of TRPV4 produced larger calcium transients in modified endothelial cells and was associated with changes in cell viability and barrier resistance, demonstrating a relationship between calcium signaling and endothelial functional responses. In 2024, a study involving human corneal endothelial cells evaluated calcium signals in propagated cells in vitro as a potential functional quality-assessment method for cell therapy. In contrast, in vivo corneal calcium-imaging studies have focused mainly on non-endothelial cell populations. McPheeters et al. (2024) used GCaMP6f and non-contact confocal microscopy to image calcium activity in mouse corneal nerves in vivo, demonstrating the feasibility of dynamic calcium imaging in the living mouse cornea. Similarly, Chen et al. (2024) used multiphoton microscopy to investigate injury-induced calcium responses in epithelial cells in living mouse corneas. Neither study characterized calcium dynamics in the corneal endothelium. The frequency, amplitude, temporal kinetics, and cellular distribution of calcium transients in the living mammalian corneal endothelium remain poorly characterized. This study seeks to address this gap by developing and applying a computational analysis pipeline to in vivo calcium-imaging recordings from the corneal endothelium of mice to quantitatively characterize calcium-signal dynamics and determine how these dynamics vary across age groups.
